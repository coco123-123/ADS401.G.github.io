CMO AI: Campus Information Retrievaland Open-ended Campus Planning

This study compares Baseline and CMO AI information-seeking conditions in campus information retrieval and open-ended planning, focusing on efficiency, accuracy, service discovery, information coverage and actionability.

Background and Research Questions

Campus service information is distributed across official websites, apps, WeChat accounts and departmental pages. This study examines whether CMO AI can answer explicit questions and help students discover campus services and planning requirements they have not yet considered.

RQ1 · Simple Information Retrieval

Does CMO AI improve the efficiency and accuracy of campus information retrieval?

Compare task completion time, completion status and answer accuracy between the Baseline and CMO AI conditions.

RQ2 · Open-ended Campus Planning

Can CMO AI help students discover and integrate the campus services required for open-ended planning?

The evaluation focuses on service discovery, information coverage, completeness, accuracy and actionability.

Target Users

The target population is newly enrolled XJTLU students, including:

  • First-year undergraduate students
  • First-year postgraduate students

New students have limited campus experience and may be unfamiliar with service entry points, application procedures and responsible departments.

Study objective: evaluate the assistance provided by CMO AI and identify information and procedures that require further verification.

Research Rationale

The following four points bring together the motivation, target users, comparison conditions and task categories in the existing proposal.

Why Study CMO AI?

New students may not know where to find campus information, which department is responsible or which steps a service requires. Through natural-language interaction, CMO AI may help them retrieve and integrate campus service information.

Why this matters: campus information is distributed across multiple sources.

Why Focus on New Students?

New students have limited familiarity with campus services and may face unclear entry points, uncertain procedures and difficulty discovering relevant services.

Target group: first-year undergraduate and postgraduate students.

Why Compare Baseline and CMO AI?

The Baseline group uses existing campus information channels. The CMO AI group can also use CMO AI. This comparison is intended to estimate the additional value of access to CMO AI.

Rationale for the between-subjects factor.

Why Include Two Task Types?

Simple tasks assess retrieval when users already know what they need. Complex tasks assess whether users discover services they have not considered and integrate information into an actionable plan.

Rationale for the within-subjects task-type factor.

Experimental Design and Implementation Plan

The study uses a 2 × 2 mixed design. Information-seeking condition is the between-subjects factor, with Baseline and CMO AI levels. Task type is the within-subjects factor, with Simple retrieval and Complex open-ended planning levels. Each participant uses one information-seeking condition and completes both task types.

Variable Definitions and Research Hypotheses

H1 · Main Effect of Information-seeking Condition

Better Performance with CMO AI

Compared with Baseline, the CMO AI group is expected to have shorter completion times, higher completion rates and greater accuracy.

H2 · Interaction Effect

Greater Assistance for Complex Tasks

CMO AI is expected to provide greater benefits for service discovery, information coverage, completeness and actionability in Complex open-ended planning than in Simple tasks.

A pilot study will check the feasibility of task difficulty, time limits and scoring criteria. Findings must be based on the data actually collected.

Sampling method Convenience samplingRecruitment channels New-student groups, student communities and campus social platformsAssignment plan Random assignment; approximately 30 participants per group

Proposed Experimental Procedure

  1. Study Information and Informed Consent

    Explain the purpose, procedure, data collection, withdrawal rights and anonymisation arrangements.

  2. Pre-study Questionnaire and Random Assignment

    Record subject area, campus information-seeking channels and difficulties, prior AI experience and availability. Randomly assign participants to a condition.

  3. Six Tasks and Task-block Questionnaires

    Complete three simple tasks (S1–S3) and three complex tasks (C1–C3). Record time, completion status and submitted outputs for each task. Collect block questionnaires after S1–S3 and C1–C3, followed by the overall questionnaire.

  4. Answer Scoring and Issue Recording

    Evaluate outputs using official information, reference answers and scoring sheets. Record omissions, procedural errors and source issues.

Six Experimental Tasks: S1–S3 and C1–C3

S1Student One-stop Contact Information

You need advice on a campus matter but are unsure which department to contact. Find the contact and office information for the Student One-stop service. Provide the telephone number, office location and publicly available service hours.

S2Overnight Study Room Information

You plan to study on campus during an exam-week evening. Find the usage requirements, opening arrangements and booking channel for overnight study rooms. Provide the requirements, opening hours or arrangements, and booking entry point.

S3Shuttle Services between Taicang and SIP

You need to travel between the Taicang and SIP campuses. Consult the official shuttle timetable and provide departure times and booking rules.

C1Open-ended Planning for a Day on Campus

You are a newly enrolled XJTLU student planning a Saturday on campus: study in a suitable place in the morning, play badminton with friends in the afternoon and hold a group discussion with three classmates in the evening. You are unfamiliar with campus services and want to prepare in advance so that the day runs smoothly.

Prepare an actionable schedule and find the campus services, requirements and procedures needed to carry it out. Explain the basis for your arrangements and identify matters that need advance confirmation.

C2Cross-service Planning for an External Speaker Event

Your student group plans to invite an external speaker to XJTLU next month for an afternoon talk attended by approximately 30 students. The event includes a presentation and student questions and will last about two hours. As the main organiser, you are unfamiliar with the relevant campus procedures and want to prepare in advance.

Prepare an actionable event preparation plan and find the campus services, requirements and procedures needed to implement it. Explain the basis for your arrangements and identify matters that need advance confirmation.

C3Welcome Activity Planning for New Students

You are organising a welcome activity for new students. Prepare the required campus arrangements, covering the venue, transport, catering and service support.

Evaluation Methods and Data Types

1. Objective task performance

Record completion time, completion status and answer accuracy. Compare Baseline and CMO AI on simple retrieval tasks S1–S3, and record completion times for C1–C3.

Key data: seconds, Completed / Partial / Failed, and accuracy scores.

2. Complex-task quality

Compare service discovery, information coverage, completeness, accuracy and actionability in the final C1–C3 plans against independently verified official reference answers.

Key data: number of services discovered, required-information coverage, completeness and actionability.

3. Subjective and qualitative evaluation

Use post-task questionnaires, open-ended responses, semi-structured interviews and participant comments to explain why participants add, revise or discard information.

Key data: block ratings, overall questionnaire responses and interview themes.

4. Query process and reasons

Use semi-structured interviews, participant comments and available AI interaction records to understand why participants select, revise, verify or discard information.

Key data: search strategies, official-source verification, AI interaction patterns and error types.

Semi-structured Interview Guide

The five draft interview questions cover time spent on tasks, information verification, conflicting sources, complex-task information integration and priorities for improving campus AI.

Targeted Interview Questions

Draft prompts
  1. 1. Time Spent on Tasks

    Which tasks took you the most time? Was most of that time spent finding information, judging whether it was trustworthy, or organising your answer?

  2. 2. Currency and Applicability of Information

    How did you judge whether the information was up to date and applicable to XJTLU? Did you check the source, date or page hierarchy, or rely on prior experience?

  3. 3. Conflicting Information

    If CMO and the official website provided conflicting information, which would you trust? How would you like CMO to display information sources or update dates?

    In complex tasks, which information was hardest to integrate: venues, procedures, responsible departments, required documents, contact details or scheduling?

  5. 5. Priorities for Improvement

    What do you think is the most important improvement needed for a campus service AI assistant?

Study Implementation: Reflection and Limitations

This section summarises anticipated implementation issues and planned improvements. Empirical findings will be added after data collection.

  • Implementation of the Study Plan

    Record changes to the planned procedure and relevant participant feedback during the pilot and formal study.

  • Variable Control and Potential AI Contamination

    AI-generated search summaries may affect the Baseline condition. Standardise devices, disable other AI features where possible and record unavoidable exposure.

  • Task Design, Scoring and Measurement

    Use verified answers for simple tasks. For complex tasks, apply a reference checklist and assess information coverage and accuracy.

  • Scope of the Findings

    Convenience sampling, new-student participants and selected campus tasks limit how widely the findings can be generalised.

Key Limitations and Planned Improvements

These issues will be checked during the pilot and formal study.

1. Potential AI Contamination
Search engines may expose the Baseline group to AI-generated content, so the search environment must be controlled.
2. Ambiguity in Complex-task Scoring
Complex tasks have no single correct answer. Use a verified checklist, explicit scoring rules and two raters where feasible.
3. Limited Evidence of User Behaviour
Completion time cannot explain user strategy. Use task records, interaction logs and short interviews for additional context.
